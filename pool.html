01 / the liquidity problem

what people
complain about.

stuff people actually say
about memecoin pools.

  1. "look at the pool before the price."everyone watches the price. the pool decides what you get when you sell.
  2. "the quote was optimistic."you hit sell and got less than the screen said.
  3. "many wallets. one guy."the holder count looks big. it's one guy with a lot of wallets.
  4. "fees had somewhere else to be."the volume was real. the fees went to a wallet nobody checked.
  5. "lp pulled."the pool was there monday. tuesday someone pulled the lp and it wasn't.
  6. "40% slippage."the pool was so thin that selling cost you almost half.
  7. "exit liquidity."someone needed a buyer so they could sell. it was you.
  8. "graduated. still needs supervision."the coin left the bonding curve and landed in a tiny pool.

duk can't fix any of that.
he can put his creator fees in the pool.

02 / the plumbing

how duk's
pool works.

five steps.
a bot does them.

  1. 1creator fees claimedthe keeper claims duk's creator fees into the fee wallet.
  2. 2buy duka slice of the sol buys duk on the pool.
  3. 3pair with solthe duk it bought + the sol it didn't spend.
  4. 4add to the poolboth go into the duk/sol pumpswap pool.
  5. 5burn the lp from that addthe lp tokens from the bot's add get burned. burned lp can't be redeemed, so nobody can withdraw that liquidity, us included.

the fine print, in big letters.

  • it runs every batch. duk graduated to pumpswap on nov 26. since then every batch goes in: fees claimed, a slice buys duk, duk and sol added, lp burned. it never sells duk.
  • 4% stays in the wallet. the bot keeps 4% of supply (40m duk) as a reserve. it never sells it and never puts it in the pool. the reserve, live.
  • a keeper does it. a small bot on a server. it checks on a timer and works in batches. thresholds and a small sol reserve apply.
  • operating costs come out before the split. network fees and running costs first. then the reserve refill if it's below 4%, then the slice that buys duk, then the add.
  • additions depend on fees, the keeper and execution. no trades means no fees, and no fees means nothing to add. if the keeper is down, nothing gets added either.
  • other people's lp is separate. we burn the lp from our adds. everyone else's lp is theirs.

05 / the limits

what it is.
what it isn't.

read the second list
before you buy.

it is

  • creator fees helping deepen the pool.
  • the lp from each add, burned.
  • receipts in public. every add links to its transaction.

it isn't

  • a price floor. price can still go down.
  • a guarantee of anything.
  • control over other people's lp.
  • a burn of duk tokens. the duk it buys goes into the pool, or into the 4% reserve while that's refilling. only the lp tokens get burned.
  • a reason to wash trade. buying and selling with yourself to make fees just costs you the fees.

06 / questions from the shallow end

pool faq.

short answers.

why burn the lp?

lp tokens are the claim ticket to take liquidity back out. the keeper burns the lp from the bot's adds. burned lp can't be redeemed, so nobody can withdraw that liquidity, us included. selling can still take sol out of the pool.

does this pump the price?

it isn't built to. each batch buys some duk and puts it back in the pool next to sol. while the reserve is below 4%, part of the batch buys duk that stays in the wallet. the point is depth: a deeper pool can mean less slippage. price can still go down. it's not a price floor.

what if nobody trades?

then there are no fees and nothing to add. the counters stay where they are.

who runs the keeper?

the dev runs it: a small automated service on a server. it claims creator fees, does the swap, the add and the burn, and writes the log this page reads.

can you stop it?

yes. it's a program on a server, and servers can stop. if it stops, this page says so and the counter stops moving. lp it already burned stays burned.

where are the receipts?

up there. every add in the table links to its transaction on solscan. the fee wallet is public too.
